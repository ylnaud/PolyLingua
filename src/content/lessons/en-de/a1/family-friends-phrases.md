Talking about family and friends is one of the earliest, most natural conversation
topics you will have in German. This lesson covers the core vocabulary and how to
introduce people using possessives like "mein" and "meine".

## Core family words

| German | English |
| -------- | ------- |
| die Familie | the family |
| die Eltern | the parents |
| die Mutter | the mother |
| der Vater | the father |
| der Bruder | the brother |
| die Schwester | the sister |
| die Geschwister | the siblings |
| der Sohn / die Tochter | the son / the daughter |
| die Großmutter / der Großvater | the grandmother / the grandfather |
| die Tante / der Onkel | the aunt / the uncle |

Notice **die Geschwister** has no direct English translation — it means "siblings" as a
group, brothers and sisters together, and it is always used in the plural.

## "Mein" and "meine": possessives match the noun's gender

To say "my [family member]", the word for "my" changes depending on whether the noun is
masculine/neuter or feminine:

```
mein Bruder     (der Bruder is masculine → mein)
mein Kind       (das Kind is neuter → mein, same as masculine here)
meine Schwester (die Schwester is feminine → meine)
meine Familie   (die Familie is feminine → meine)
```

The rule of thumb at this level: masculine and neuter nouns take **mein**, feminine
nouns take **meine**. You will learn the fuller possessive pattern (for plural and other
cases) later — for now, this covers the vast majority of everyday sentences.

## Friend or something more? Freund/Freundin

German uses the same word for "friend" and "romantic partner", and context does the
work of telling them apart:

- Das ist ein Freund von mir. — This is a friend of mine. (using "ein" signals just a friend)
- Das ist mein Freund. — This is my boyfriend. (using "mein" without qualifiers often implies boyfriend)

To be unambiguous about a platonic friend, German speakers sometimes add "guter" (good):
Das ist ein guter Freund von mir. (This is a good friend of mine.)

## Sample sentences

- Meine Familie ist groß. — My family is big.
- Ich habe eine Schwester und einen Bruder. — I have a sister and a brother.
- Meine Eltern wohnen in München. — My parents live in Munich.
- Das ist meine beste Freundin. — This is my best friend. (female)

Try introducing your own family members out loud using "Das ist mein/meine..." — it is
one of the most common things you will actually say to new people in German.
