Food vocabulary is some of the most practical German you'll ever learn — you'll use it
every day, whether you're ordering at a restaurant, reading a menu, or shopping at a
market.

## Everyday staples

| German        | English     |
| ------------- | ----------- |
| das Brot      | bread       |
| das Wasser    | water       |
| der Kaffee    | coffee      |
| der Tee       | tea         |
| die Milch     | milk        |

## A pattern worth noticing: basic foods lean neuter

It's not a hard rule the way -chen always means neuter, but it's a useful tendency:
many everyday, "basic" food categories are neuter (das): das Brot (bread), das Obst
(fruit), das Gemüse (vegetables), das Fleisch (meat), das Ei (egg). Specific individual
food items, by contrast, are more mixed — der Apfel (apple) is masculine, die Banane
(banana) is feminine, der Käse (cheese) is masculine. As always, learn each word with
its article rather than relying on the tendency alone.

## Fruits, vegetables, and proteins

| German        | English      |
| ------------- | ------------ |
| der Apfel     | apple         |
| das Obst      | fruit (general) |
| das Gemüse    | vegetables (general) |
| das Fleisch   | meat          |
| der Fisch     | fish          |
| der Käse      | cheese        |
| das Ei        | egg           |
| der Saft      | juice         |

Note that "Käse" (cheese) ends in -e, which usually signals a feminine noun — but it's
one of a small group of common masculine exceptions (alongside der Name, der Kunde),
so it's worth memorizing on its own.

## The three meals of the day

| German           | English    |
| ----------------- | ---------- |
| das Frühstück      | breakfast  |
| das Mittagessen    | lunch      |
| das Abendessen     | dinner     |

Notice the compound structure: "Mittag" (midday) + "Essen" (eating/food) =
Mittagessen. "Abend" (evening) + "Essen" = Abendessen. Once you know "das Essen" means
"the food/eating," you can recognize the pattern in dozens of related compound words.

## Ordering and talking about food

A few useful everyday phrases to attach to this vocabulary:

> Ich möchte ein Brot, bitte. (I would like a bread, please.)
> Ich trinke gern Kaffee am Morgen. (I like to drink coffee in the morning.)
> Zum Frühstück esse ich ein Ei. (For breakfast I eat an egg.)

"Ich möchte..." (I would like...) and "Ich esse/trinke gern..." (I like to eat/drink...)
are two frames you can reuse endlessly by simply swapping in any food word from this
lesson.
