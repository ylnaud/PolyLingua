Every language starts the same way: with hello. German has more greeting options than
English, but they follow a simple, logical pattern once you see it laid out.

## Greetings by time of day

German greetings are tied to the clock more strictly than English "good morning" is.
Picture the day split into three zones:

| German         | English         | When to use it              |
| -------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Guten Morgen   | Good morning    | Waking up until around noon  |
| Guten Tag      | Good day        | Noon until early evening     |
| Guten Abend    | Good evening    | From early evening onward    |

All three are polite and safe to use with strangers, shopkeepers, or coworkers. If you
are unsure which zone you are in, "Guten Tag" is the safest all-purpose choice during
daylight hours.

## The casual option: Hallo

If greetings-by-clock feel like too much to track, "Hallo" is your escape hatch. It
works at any hour and in almost any informal situation — the direct equivalent of
English "hi." You will hear it constantly among friends, classmates, and in casual
shops.

## Saying goodbye

Just as there is a formal/informal split for hello, there is one for goodbye:

- **Auf Wiedersehen** — formal, literally "until we see each other again." Use it with
  strangers, in shops, or in professional settings.
- **Tschüss** — informal and friendly, the equivalent of "bye." Use it with people you
  already know.

## Introducing yourself

Once you have greeted someone, the natural next step is to say who you are. Two
phrases cover almost every self-introduction:

- **Wie heißt du?** (What is your name?) → **Ich heiße...** (My name is...)
- **Woher kommst du?** (Where are you from?) → **Ich komme aus...** (I come from...)

Notice that "heißen" literally means "to be called" — Germans don't say "I am Anna,"
they say "I am called Anna" (Ich heiße Anna). Both work fine in conversation, but
"Ich heiße" is the more natural, textbook-standard phrasing.

## Asking how someone is doing

"Wie geht es dir?" (How are you?) is the informal way to check in with someone. A
typical reply is "Mir geht's gut, danke" (I'm doing well, thanks), or you can simply
flip the question back with "Und dir?" (And you?).

## Putting it together

A first meeting between two people who already know to use "du" (informal "you") might
sound like this:

> — Hallo! Wie heißt du?
> — Ich heiße Paul. Und du?
> — Ich heiße Sofia. Woher kommst du?
> — Ich komme aus Spanien.

Learn this exchange by heart — it is the single most-used script in beginner German,
and you will reuse every piece of it constantly as you build up your vocabulary.
