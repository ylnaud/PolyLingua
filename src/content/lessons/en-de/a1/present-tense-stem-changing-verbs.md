Some of the most common German verbs break the neat regular pattern you just learned —
but only in a small, predictable way. A group of verbs change their stem vowel, and
only in two out of six forms.

## The rule: only du and er/sie/es change

Stem-changing verbs keep the normal regular endings (-e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en), but
in the **du** and **er/sie/es** forms, the vowel inside the stem itself shifts. Every
other form — ich, wir, ihr, sie/Sie — stays completely regular.

## Pattern 1: a → ä (fahren)

| Pronoun       | fahren   |
| ------------- | -------- |
| ich           | fahre    |
| du            | **fährst** |
| er / sie / es | **fährt**  |
| wir           | fahren   |
| ihr           | fahrt    |
| sie / Sie     | fahren   |

Only du and er/sie/es pick up the umlaut: fahre → fährst / fährt.

## Pattern 2: e → i (essen)

| Pronoun       | essen    |
| ------------- | -------- |
| ich           | esse     |
| du            | **isst**   |
| er / sie / es | **isst**   |
| wir           | essen    |
| ihr           | esst     |
| sie / Sie     | essen    |

Here the short e becomes a short i, and because the stem already ends in -s, adding
another -s(t) collapses into a single "isst" for both du and er/sie/es.

## Pattern 3: e → ie (sehen, lesen)

| Pronoun       | sehen    | lesen    |
| ------------- | -------- | -------- |
| ich           | sehe     | lese     |
| du            | **siehst** | **liest**  |
| er / sie / es | **sieht**  | **liest**  |
| wir           | sehen    | lesen    |
| ihr           | seht     | lest     |
| sie / Sie     | sehen    | lesen    |

The long e becomes "ie" (pronounced like a long "ee") in exactly the same two forms.
Notice that lesen's du-form and er/sie/es-form are identical ("liest") — that overlap
is common with e → ie verbs.

## Why memorizing these four matters

Fahren, essen, sehen, and lesen are among the most frequently used verbs in everyday
German, so their irregular du/er forms come up constantly — in questions ("Was isst
du?" What are you eating?), in small talk ("Siehst du das?" Do you see that?), and in
daily routines ("Er liest die Zeitung." He reads the newspaper.). Because the
vowel-change forms don't follow the regular pattern, there is no shortcut except
practice: say each pair — fährst/fährt, isst/isst, siehst/sieht, liest/liest — out
loud until they feel automatic.

## The good news

Once you're past du and er/sie/es, every other form of these verbs is completely
regular. Ich fahre, wir fahren, ihr fahrt, sie fahren — no surprises there at all.
