Three letter combinations cause most of the pronunciation trouble for English speakers
learning German: **sch**, **ch**, and **ck**. The first and third are simple. The
middle one has a rule most textbooks bury in a footnote — this lesson puts it front and
center.

## sch — always "sh"

This one is easy: "sch" is just the German spelling for the English "sh" sound, as in
"shoe" or "ship". It never changes.

```
Schule    "SHOO-luh"    school
Fisch     "fish"        fish (almost identical to English)
schön     "shern"       beautiful (you met this one in the umlaut lesson)
```

## ck — a short, hard "k"

"ck" behaves almost exactly like it does in English: it is a plain hard "k" sound, and
its main job is to signal that the vowel right before it is **short**.

```
dick      "dick"        thick / fat
Zucker    "TSU-ker"     sugar
Rock      "rock"        skirt (a "false friend" — it does not mean rock music!)
```

## ch — two different sounds, one spelling

This is the one that actually needs a rule. "ch" is not one sound in German — it is
**two**, and which one you use depends entirely on the letter right before it.

### The soft "ich-sound" (front of the mouth)

Used after the front vowels **e, i, ä, ö, ü**, and after consonants like **n, l, r**.
It is an airy, breathy hiss made at the front of the mouth — the closest English
approximation is saying "huge" or "human" very breathily, pushing air through instead
of a real h.

```
ich      "I"                    the soft hiss, not a hard k
Milch    "milk"
manche   "some"
```

### The rough "ach-sound" (back of the throat)

Used after the back vowels **a, o, u**. This is a rougher, throat-clearing sound, made
in the back of the throat — the same sound as the Scottish "loch" (not "lock").

```
ach      "oh / ah"     (an exclamation)
Buch     "book"
auch     "also / too"
```

### Quick test: which one do I use?

| Letter right before "ch" | Sound      | Example        |
| ------------------------- | ---------- | -------------- |
| e, i, ä, ö, ü, or a consonant | soft ich-sound  | ich, Milch |
| a, o, u                    | rough ach-sound | Buch, auch |

## A common beginner mistake

English speakers, seeing "ch", often default to a hard "k" sound out of habit —
saying "ick" instead of the soft "ich-sound" for "ich" (I). Resist that instinct. It
should be airy and light, not a hard consonant at all.

## How to practice

Say this pair back to back, exaggerating the difference:

```
ich (soft, front of mouth)   ...   auch (rough, back of throat)
```

If you can feel the sound move from the front of your mouth to the back of your
throat between those two words, you have both ch-sounds under control.
