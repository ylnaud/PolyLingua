If you have never seen ä, ö, or ü before, here is the first thing to understand: those
two dots are called an **umlaut**, and they are not optional styling. Drop them and you
get a different word.

```
schon  =  already        schön  =  beautiful
```

English speakers often try to guess these sounds "by ear," copying what they hear.
That works for ä, but it usually fails for ö and ü — English simply does not have
those two vowel sounds. The trick that actually works is **mechanical**: it is about
where you put your lips, not what you think the vowel should sound like.

## ä — the easy one

ä is the closest of the three to a sound you already make in English. It sounds like
the "e" in **bed**, **there**, or **when** — an open "eh" — sometimes held a bit longer
than the English version.

```
Käse     "KEH-zuh"     cheese
Bär      "BEHR"        bear
Mädchen  "MEHT-chen"   girl
```

## ö and ü — sounds English does not have

Here is the trick: **pick the lip shape of one English vowel, but say a different
English vowel through it.**

```
ö  →  round your lips as if you were about to say "oh" — then say "eh"
ü  →  round your lips as if you were about to say "oo" (as in "moon") — then say "ee"
```

Try it in front of a mirror. For ö:

```
Öl        "royally rounded 'oh' lips, but the sound 'eh' comes out"   oil
schön     lips for "oh", sound "eh"                                   beautiful
```

And for ü, the lips are tighter and more forward than for ö:

```
Tür       lips for "oo", sound "ee"     door
Prüfung   lips for "oo", sound "ee"     exam
```

Put a finger lightly on your lips while you practice. If they flatten out into a smile
shape, the sound is wrong — for both ö and ü, the lips have to stay rounded the entire
time you are making the vowel.

## A trap: "äu" is not "ä"

When ä is followed by u, the whole sound changes into a diphthong that has nothing to
do with ä on its own. "äu" (like its close relative "eu") is pronounced like the **"oy"
in "boy"**:

```
Mäuse    "MOY-zuh"    mice
Häuser   "HOY-zer"    houses
Bäume    "BOY-muh"    trees
```

This combination shows up constantly in German plurals: `Haus → Häuser`,
`Baum → Bäume`, `Maus → Mäuse`. If you see äu, do not reach for the ä sound — reach for
"oy".

## How to practice

Say the pair **schon / schön** out loud several times in a row, exaggerating the lip
rounding on the second one. Once you can clearly hear the two as different words, you
have the sound — the rest is just repeating it with new vocabulary. Always learn German
nouns with their article attached (der Löffel, not just "Löffel") — you will meet why
that matters in the next unit.
