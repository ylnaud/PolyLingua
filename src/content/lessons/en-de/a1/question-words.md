Six little words unlock most of the questions you will ever need to ask in German.
Germans call them **W-Fragen** (W-questions), because every single one starts with the
letter W: **wer, was, wo, wann, warum, wie**.

## The six W-Fragen

| German | English | Example                              |
| ------ | ------- | ------------------------------------- |
| wer    | who     | Wer ist das? (Who is that?)           |
| was    | what    | Was machst du? (What are you doing?)  |
| wo     | where   | Wo wohnst du? (Where do you live?)    |
| wann   | when    | Wann kommst du? (When are you coming?)|
| warum  | why     | Warum lachst du? (Why are you laughing?)|
| wie    | how     | Wie geht es dir? (How are you?)       |

Notice the pattern in the English translations too: who, what, where, when, why, how —
English question words mostly start with "wh," and German ones mostly start with "w."
That shared ancestry (both languages descend from a common Germanic root) makes these
six words easier to remember than most German vocabulary.

## Word order: question word, then verb

German W-questions follow a strict, simple pattern:

**question word + verb + subject (+ rest of sentence)**

- **Wo** **wohnst** du? (Where do you live?)
- **Wann** **kommt** er? (When is he coming?)
- **Warum** **lernst** du Deutsch? (Why are you learning German?)

The verb always comes immediately after the question word — never later in the
sentence, the way it sometimes can in casual English ("Where you at?"). Once you get
used to putting the verb second, every W-question you build will sound natural.

## Two useful extras

Beyond the core six, two more W-words are worth knowing early:

- **wie viel** (how much) — Wie viel kostet das? (How much does that cost?)
- **welche / welcher / welches** (which) — Welches Buch möchtest du? (Which book would
  you like?) — this one changes its ending to match the gender of the noun, similar to
  der/die/das.

## Common mix-up: wie vs. wer

English speakers sometimes confuse "wie" (how) with "wer" (who) because they look and
sound similar. A quick trick: "wer" rhymes with "her," and both ask about a **person**.
"Wie" asks about **manner** — how something is done or how someone feels.

## Try building your own

Once you know the six W-words and the "question word + verb" order, you can ask about
almost anything:

> Wer bist du? Was machst du hier? Wo ist der Bahnhof? Wann beginnt der Film?
> Warum ist das so teuer? Wie funktioniert das?

Practice swapping the verb and subject while keeping the question word first — that
single habit is what makes German questions sound correct.
