Whether you are buying groceries, souvenirs, or clothes, a handful of shopping phrases
will get you through almost any store in a German-speaking country. This lesson focuses
on the words and questions you will actually use at the register and on the shop floor.

## The most important question: Wie viel kostet das?

If you learn only one shopping phrase, make it this one:

- **Wie viel kostet das?** — How much does that cost?

You can point at anything and ask this question, even before you know the name of the
item. The reply will usually be a number followed by "Euro":

- Das kostet zehn Euro. — That costs ten euros.

## Verbs you need at a store

| German | English |
| -------- | ------- |
| einkaufen | to go shopping |
| kaufen | to buy |
| bezahlen | to pay |
| kosten | to cost |
| möchten | would like (polite "want") |

A very common, polite sentence combines möchte with kaufen at the end:

- Ich möchte das kaufen. — I would like to buy this.
- Ich möchte bezahlen. — I would like to pay. (said at the register)

## Describing price

| German | English |
| -------- | ------- |
| teuer | expensive |
| günstig | cheap / good value |
| billig | cheap (can sound like "cheaply made") |
| kostenlos | free of charge |

Note the small difference between günstig and billig: günstig is a neutral, positive way
to say something is a good deal, while billig can sometimes hint that something is low
quality — a distinction worth knowing even at a beginner level.

## Places and things

| German | English |
| -------- | ------- |
| der Supermarkt | supermarket |
| das Geschäft | store / shop |
| die Kasse | cash register / checkout |
| das Geld | money |
| die Rechnung | the receipt / bill |

## Sample conversation at a shop

```
A: Kann ich Ihnen helfen?          Can I help you?
B: Ja, wie viel kostet das Buch?    Yes, how much does the book cost?
A: Das kostet zwölf Euro.           That costs twelve euros.
B: Gut, ich möchte das kaufen.       Good, I would like to buy this.
```

Practice this exchange out loud with different prices and items — swapping in vocabulary
from other lessons (das Buch, das Auto, der Tisch) is a quick way to build confidence.
