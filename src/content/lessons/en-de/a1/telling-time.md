German has two separate systems for telling time, and knowing when to use each one
matters as much as knowing the words themselves.

## Asking the time

The standard question is **"Wie spät ist es?"** — literally "How late is it?" You will
also hear "Wie viel Uhr ist es?" (What hour is it?), which means the same thing.

## System 1: Formal — the 24-hour clock

Official contexts — train and flight schedules, TV listings, appointments, business —
use the 24-hour clock directly, read out as plain numbers followed by "Uhr" (o'clock):

| Time  | German               |
| ----- | -------------------- |
| 9:00  | neun Uhr              |
| 14:00 | vierzehn Uhr           |
| 14:30 | vierzehn Uhr dreißig   |
| 20:15 | zwanzig Uhr fünfzehn   |

This system exists because German has no simple AM/PM marker the way English does —
"vierzehn Uhr" can only mean 2 PM, removing any ambiguity. As a beginner, this is
actually the *safer* system to lean on, since it never requires guessing.

## System 2: Informal — halb, Viertel, vor, nach

In everyday spoken conversation, Germans normally use a 12-hour system built from four
key words:

- **Viertel** (quarter)
- **halb** (half)
- **vor** (to/before)
- **nach** (past/after)

Two of the four pieces — Viertel nach and Viertel vor — work exactly like English:

| German             | Meaning       |
| ------------------- | ------------- |
| Viertel nach vier    | 4:15 (quarter past four) |
| Viertel vor acht     | 7:45 (quarter to eight)  |

## The trap: halb does NOT mean "half past"

This is the single biggest mistake English speakers make with German time. In English,
"half past three" means 3:30 — you're counting forward from three. German "halb drei"
also means 2:30 — but it's counting **forward to three**, not backward from it. Think
of "halb drei" as "halfway to three o'clock":

```
halb drei   = 2:30  (halfway TO 3:00, not "half past 2")
halb sieben = 6:30  (halfway TO 7:00)
halb zwölf  = 11:30 (halfway TO 12:00)
```

If you mentally translate "halb drei" as "half past three," you will consistently be
an hour off. Always read "halb [hour]" as "half TO that hour."

## Two more useful words

- **Mittag** — noon (12:00 PM)
- **Mitternacht** — midnight (12:00 AM)

## Putting it together

> — Wie spät ist es?
> — Es ist halb drei. (It's 2:30.)
> — Und wann beginnt der Film?
> — Um Viertel nach acht. (At 8:15.)

If you ever feel unsure whether "halb" applies, fall back on the 24-hour system — "Es
ist vierzehn Uhr dreißig" is always correct and always understood, even if it sounds a
touch more formal than the everyday version.
