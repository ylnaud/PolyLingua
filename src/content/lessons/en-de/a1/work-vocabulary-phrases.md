Talking about work is one of the first topics that comes up in almost any conversation
with a new acquaintance in a German-speaking country. This lesson gives you the core
vocabulary and the sentence patterns to describe your job.

## The key question: Was machst du beruflich?

The natural way to ask about someone's profession is:

- Was machst du beruflich? — What do you do for a living? (informal)
- Was machen Sie beruflich? — What do you do for a living? (formal)

To answer, you have two common patterns:

1. **Ich bin [job title].** — Ich bin Lehrer. (I am a teacher.)
2. **Ich arbeite als [job title].** — Ich arbeite als Lehrer. (I work as a teacher.)

## The no-article rule

Here is the pattern that trips up a lot of English speakers: German **drops the article**
before a profession, nationality, or religion when it follows sein or "als":

```
Ich bin Lehrer.        NOT   Ich bin ein Lehrer.
Ich bin Ärztin.         NOT   Ich bin eine Ärztin.
Ich arbeite als Koch.   NOT   Ich arbeite als ein Koch.
```

English always needs "a" here ("I am *a* teacher"), but German treats the profession
almost like an adjective describing you, not a countable noun — so no article appears.

## Male and female job titles

Most German job titles have a masculine form and a feminine form, usually built by
adding **-in** to the masculine version:

| Masculine | Feminine | English |
| ----------- | ---------- | -------- |
| der Lehrer | die Lehrerin | teacher |
| der Arzt | die Ärztin | doctor |
| der Ingenieur | die Ingenieurin | engineer |
| der Kollege | die Kollegin | colleague |
| der Chef | die Chefin | boss |

## Core workplace vocabulary

| German | English |
| -------- | ------- |
| der Beruf | profession / job |
| die Arbeit | work |
| das Büro | office |
| arbeiten | to work |
| der Kollege / die Kollegin | colleague |
| der Chef / die Chefin | boss |
| der Arbeitsplatz | workplace |

## Sample sentences

- Ich arbeite in einem Büro. — I work in an office.
- Meine Chefin ist sehr nett. — My boss (female) is very nice.
- Wir haben heute viel Arbeit. — We have a lot of work today.
- Er ist Ingenieur von Beruf. — He is an engineer by profession.

Practice describing your own job (or a job you would like to have) using both patterns —
"Ich bin..." and "Ich arbeite als..." — since both are equally natural in everyday German.
