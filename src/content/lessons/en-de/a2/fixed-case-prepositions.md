You already know that German articles change depending on the case a noun plays in a
sentence — nominative, accusative, dative. Prepositions add one more layer: a lot of
them force the noun that follows them into one specific case, always, no matter what
the sentence is about. Learn the preposition, and you already know the case. No
guessing required.

## The five that are always Akkusativ

```
durch   through
für     for
gegen   against
ohne    without
um      around / at (with clock time)
```

```
Ich laufe durch den Park.       Ich kaufe es für dich.
Ich bin gegen den Plan.         Ich gehe ohne dich.
```

Every one of these forces the noun that follows into Akkusativ, every single time:
`der` → `den`, `mein` → `meinen`, `eine` stays `eine` (feminine Akkusativ looks the
same as nominative).

## The seven that are always Dativ

```
aus     from / out of
bei     at / near
mit     with
nach    after / to (with cities/countries)
seit    since
von     from / of
zu      to
```

```
Ich komme aus der Schweiz.      Ich wohne bei meiner Oma.
Ich fahre mit dem Bus.          Ich arbeite seit einem Jahr hier.
```

These force Dativ every time: `der` → `dem` (masculine/neuter), `die` → `der`
(feminine), `mein` → `meinem`/`meiner`.

## Why this is good news

Some German prepositions (in, an, auf, über, unter...) change case depending on
whether there's movement or a fixed location — that's a separate topic ("two-way
prepositions"). The prepositions in this lesson are much simpler: **they never
change**. Once you memorize which list a preposition belongs to, the case is fixed
forever — no need to analyze the sentence.

## Quick reference table

| Akkusativ (always) | Dativ (always) |
| --- | --- |
| durch, für, gegen, ohne, um | aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu |

## How to practice

Say ten short sentences out loud, alternating one Akkusativ preposition and one Dativ
preposition each time: "Ich gehe durch den Park." / "Ich komme aus der Schweiz." The
goal isn't the vocabulary — it's training your ear to hear which case follows which
preposition, until the correct article comes out automatically.
