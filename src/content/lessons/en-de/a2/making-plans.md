Arranging a plan always follows the same four steps: propose, accept or decline,
agree on a time, and sometimes cancel.

## 1. Proposing

```
Hast du am Samstag Zeit?                     Do you have time on Saturday?
Wollen wir ins Kino gehen?                   Shall we go to the movies?
Hast du Lust, einen Kaffee zu trinken?       Do you feel like getting a coffee?
```

**`Wollen wir...?`** translated word for word would be "do we want...?", which sounds
very strange. But in German it's simply the way to propose something — the exact
equivalent of the English "shall we...?" Use it as a fixed block and you're set.

The other formula, `Hast du Lust, ... zu + infinitive`, sends the verb to the end:

```
Hast du Lust,  einen Kaffee  zu trinken?
```

## 2. Accepting or declining

```
Ja, gern!                        Yes, gladly!
Das passt mir gut.               That works well for me.
Das passt mir leider nicht.      That unfortunately doesn't work for me.
Vielleicht ein anderes Mal.      Maybe another time.
```

`passen` always takes `mir/dir/ihm` (**Dativ**), just like `helfen` and `gefallen`:
`das passt **mir**`, not _"das passt mich"_.

And `leider` (unfortunately) is the word that softens any refusal. A bare `nein`
sounds blunt; `leider nicht` is the normal, polite way to say no.

## 3. Agreeing on details

```
Wann treffen wir uns?            When are we meeting?
Um acht Uhr vor dem Kino.        At eight in front of the movie theater.
Abgemacht!                       Deal!
```

## 4. Cancelling

```
Ich muss leider absagen.         I unfortunately have to cancel.
Wir sehen uns morgen.            See you tomorrow.
```

## How to practice

Rehearse the whole exchange out loud, playing both roles: one person proposes, the
other accepts, and you agree on a time. Then repeat it but decline with `Das passt mir
leider nicht`. That's six sentences that cover any plan you'd want to make.
