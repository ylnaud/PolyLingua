Money comes up in every everyday errand, and there is one German quirk worth knowing
before you need it.

## The question you should ask first

```
Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?      Can I pay by card?
— Nur Bargeld, bitte.            Cash only, please.
```

In Germany **there are still plenty of places that only accept cash**: bars,
bakeries, market stalls. Ask before you order, not after you have already eaten.

## Withdrawing money

```
Ich muss Geld abheben.        I need to withdraw money.
Wo ist ein Geldautomat?       Where is an ATM?
Ich habe kein Geld dabei.     I don't have any money on me.
Können Sie wechseln?          Do you have change?
```

Look at the first one: `abheben` is separable, but here it goes **entirely at the
end** because of the modal verb (`muss`). Without the modal it would be `Ich hebe Geld
ab`.

## Talking about what you can and cannot afford

```
Das ist mir zu teuer.               That's too expensive for me.
Das kann ich mir nicht leisten.     I can't afford that.
```

`sich etwas leisten` (to be able to afford something) takes the pronoun in **Dativ**:
`ich kann **mir**`, `du kannst **dir**`. This is one of those phrases worth
memorizing as a whole block.

## Income and expenses

```
Ich verdiene 2000 Euro im Monat.    I earn 2000 euros a month.
Ich spare für ein Auto.             I'm saving up for a car.
Ich habe die Miete schon bezahlt.   I already paid the rent.
Wie viel schulde ich dir?           How much do I owe you?
```

## How to practice

Memorize **`Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?`** first — it's the one you'll use most, and it
saves you the awkward moment of finding out cards aren't accepted after you've already
eaten. Then add `Das kann ich mir nicht leisten`, which works for shutting down any
sales pitch.
