You already know that der/die/das and ein/eine/ein carry gender and case information.
Now the last piece falls into place: what ending does the ADJECTIVE take when it sits
in front of a noun?

## The one-signal rule

German noun phrases always give exactly **one clear signal** of gender and case — not
zero, not two. That single idea explains all three declension patterns below.

## Weak declension: after der/die/das

When a definite article is present, it already signals gender/case clearly, so the
adjective can relax and take a **weak** ending — almost always just **-e** or **-en**.

```
der neue Mann       (Nominativ)
den neuen Mann       (Akkusativ)
die neue Frau        (Nominativ)
das neue Auto        (Nominativ)
```

## Strong declension: no article at all

With no article present, the adjective has nobody else to lean on — it has to signal
gender and case **by itself**, so it borrows the strong endings that mirror the
definite article's own pattern (der → -er, die → -e, das → -es, den → -en...).

```
guter Wein            (like "der" → -er, Nominativ masculine)
gutes Bier            (like "das" → -es, Nominativ neuter)
Ich trinke guten Wein. (like "den" → -en, Akkusativ masculine)
```

## Mixed declension: after ein-words

ein, kein and the possessives (mein, dein, sein...) mostly behave like weak
declension — **except** in the two spots where "ein" itself carries no ending at all:
masculine Nominativ, and neuter Nominativ/Akkusativ. In exactly those gaps, the
adjective has to step in and take the strong ending instead.

```
ein neuer Mann        (ein has no ending → adjective takes strong -er)
ein neues Auto         (ein has no ending → adjective takes strong -es)
einen neuen Mann       (Akkusativ: "einen" already signals case clearly → weak -en)
```

## Quick comparison table (Nominativ)

| Gender    | Weak (der/die/das) | Mixed (ein-word)      | Strong (no article) |
| --------- | ------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| maskulin  | der neue Mann        | ein neuer Mann           | guter Wein            |
| feminin   | die neue Frau         | eine neue Frau            | gute Suppe             |
| neutral   | das neue Auto         | ein neues Auto             | gutes Bier              |

This table looks like a lot to memorize at once — but if you keep asking "does an
article already give a clear signal here?", the right ending becomes far more
predictable than it first appears.
