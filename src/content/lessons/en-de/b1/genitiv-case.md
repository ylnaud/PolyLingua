German has four cases, and by now you have used Nominativ, Akkusativ and Dativ many
times. The Genitiv is the last piece of the puzzle — and the good news is it works a
lot like English possessives already do.

## What the Genitiv does: possession

The Genitiv marks that something **belongs to** someone or something:

```
das Auto meines Vaters   (my father's car)
das Haus der Familie     (the family's house)
```

Just like English adds -'s to "father" to get "father's", German adds an ending to
masculine and neuter nouns — but it also changes the article.

## The article table

| Case      | maskulin | feminin | neutral | Plural |
| --------- | -------- | ------- | ------- | ------ |
| Nominativ | der      | die     | das     | die    |
| Genitiv   | des      | der     | des     | der    |

Notice feminine and plural both use **der** in the Genitiv — the same word that is the
feminine Nominativ article, so context tells you which is which.

## The noun ending: -s or -es

Masculine and neuter singular nouns don't just get a new article — the noun itself
also picks up an ending:

```
der Mann  → des Mannes    (add -es: short/one-syllable nouns often prefer -es)
das Kind  → des Kindes    (add -es)
der Vater → des Vaters    (add -s: longer nouns often just take -s)
```

Feminine and plural nouns get **no extra ending at all** — only the article changes:

```
die Frau     → der Frau
die Kinder   → der Kinder
```

## Four prepositions that always take Genitiv

| Preposition | Meaning     | Example                          |
| ----------- | ----------- | --------------------------------- |
| wegen       | because of  | wegen des Regens                  |
| trotz       | despite     | trotz des Wetters                 |
| während     | during      | während der Woche                 |
| statt       | instead of  | statt des Autos nehme ich den Bus |

## A real-world shortcut: colloquial Dativ

In spoken, everyday German, many native speakers replace the Genitiv after wegen and
trotz with the Dativ: "wegen dem Regen" instead of "wegen des Regens". You will hear
this constantly. For writing, exams and anything formal, though, the Genitiv is still
the correct, expected form — so learn it properly even if you notice locals bending
the rule out loud.
