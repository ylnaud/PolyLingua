You already met wäre and hätte for polite requests. But most German verbs don't have
a natural-sounding Konjunktiv II form of their own — for those, German has one
universal workaround: **würde + infinitive**.

## The pattern

würde is conjugated to match the subject; the main verb stays in its plain infinitive
form and moves to the very end of the sentence — exactly like a modal verb sentence.

```
Ich würde das gern machen.        (I would like to do that.)
Wir würden das gern besprechen.    (We would like to discuss that.)
```

## Why this construction exists

Verbs like kommen, gehen, finden do technically have their own Konjunktiv II forms
(käme, ginge, fände) — but in everyday spoken German, these sound old-fashioned or
overly literary. Native speakers avoid them and reach for würde + infinitive instead:

```
Ich käme gern.        (grammatically fine, but sounds bookish)
Ich würde gern kommen. (what people actually say)
```

## Conjugating würde

| Person    | Form   |
| --------- | ------- |
| ich        | würde    |
| du          | würdest   |
| er/sie/es    | würde      |
| wir           | würden      |
| ihr            | würdet       |
| sie/Sie          | würden        |

## The exceptions: sein and haben keep their own forms

wäre and hätte are short and common enough that speakers still use them directly
instead of "würde sein" / "würde haben", which would actually sound wrong. Everything
else generally prefers würde + infinitive.

## Asking for compensation: useful phrases

```
Ich würde gern eine Entschädigung bekommen.
(I would like to receive compensation.)

Wir würden eine Rückerstattung erwarten.
(We would expect a refund.)

Würden Sie mir bitte helfen?
(Would you please help me?)

Das würde mich freuen.
(That would make me happy.)
```

Notice how much softer these sound than a direct demand like "Ich will eine
Rückerstattung!" — würde + infinitive is the everyday politeness tool that carries you
through almost any request, complaint or hypothetical you will need in real
conversation.
