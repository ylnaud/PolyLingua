Some German verbs come glued to a specific preposition — and that preposition often
translates into English completely differently than you would expect. There is no
shortcut around memorizing these; the good news is a handful of them cover most of
everyday conversation.

## Five essential pairs

| Verb + preposition            | Case      | Meaning                          |
| ------------------------------ | --------- | --------------------------------- |
| warten auf                      | Akkusativ | to wait for                        |
| sich freuen auf                  | Akkusativ | to look forward to (future)         |
| sich freuen über                  | Akkusativ | to be happy about (already happened) |
| sich interessieren für              | Akkusativ | to be interested in                  |
| denken an                            | Akkusativ | to think of / about                   |

```
Ich warte auf den Bus.               (I am waiting for the bus.)
Ich freue mich auf die Ferien.        (I am looking forward to the holidays.)
Ich freue mich über das Geschenk.      (I am happy about the gift.)
Ich interessiere mich für Musik.        (I am interested in music.)
Ich denke oft an meine Familie.          (I often think of my family.)
```

## auf vs. über with sich freuen: the timing test

This is the pair that trips learners up most. Ask yourself: **has it already
happened, or is it still coming?**

- Still coming → **auf**: Ich freue mich auf die Ferien. (holidays haven't started yet)
- Already happened → **über**: Ich freue mich über das Geschenk. (you already have it)

## Asking questions: people vs. things

When you ask a question with one of these verbs, German splits the pattern depending
on whether you are asking about a **person** or a **thing**.

For people, keep the preposition and question word separate:

```
Auf wen wartest du?      (Who(m) are you waiting for?)
An wen denkst du?         (Who(m) are you thinking of?)
```

For things, German fuses **wo-** + preposition into a single word:

```
Worauf wartest du?        (What are you waiting for?)
Wofür interessierst du dich?  (What are you interested in?)
```

## Avoiding repetition: da-compounds

The same fusion trick works to avoid repeating a thing you already mentioned. Instead
of repeating the noun, German fuses **da-** + preposition:

```
Der Bus kommt gleich. Ich warte darauf.
(The bus is coming soon. I am waiting for it.)
```

For people, you still use a normal pronoun with the preposition: "Ich warte auf ihn"
(I am waiting for him) — da-compounds are only for things and ideas, never people.
