If you have ever found a German legal document or academic paper nearly unreadable despite
knowing all the individual words, **Nominalstil** is probably why. It is the practice of
expressing actions with **nouns** instead of conjugated verbs — and it is everywhere in formal
written German.

## Verbalstil vs. Nominalstil: the same idea, two shapes

- **Verbalstil** (verb-based): *Nachdem die Sitzung beendet war, gingen alle nach Hause.*
  (After the meeting had ended, everyone went home.)
- **Nominalstil** (noun-based): *Nach Beendigung der Sitzung gingen alle nach Hause.*
  (After the meeting's conclusion, everyone went home.)

Both sentences mean the same thing. The Nominalstil version replaces an entire subordinate
clause (*nachdem die Sitzung beendet war*) with a compact prepositional phrase built around a
nominalized verb: *beenden* → *die Beendigung*.

## How nominalization works

The core move is turning a verb (or adjective) into an abstract noun, most often with one of
these productive suffixes:

| Suffix | From | Nominalized form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **-ung** | beenden (to end) | die Beendigung | the ending |
| **-ung** | untersuchen (to investigate) | die Untersuchung | the investigation |
| **-heit/-keit** | krank (sick) | die Krankheit | the illness |
| **-heit/-keit** | möglich (possible) | die Möglichkeit | the possibility |
| **-e** (verb stem) | entscheiden (to decide) | die Entscheidung* | the decision |

*(entscheiden actually nominalizes with -ung too: die Entscheidung.)

The original clause's subject usually reappears as a **genitive attribute** (or, where a
genitive would be awkward, a *von*-phrase): *die Sitzung* (subject) becomes *der Sitzung*
(genitive) in *die Beendigung der Sitzung*.

## Converting a whole clause

A subordinate clause collapses into a prepositional phrase governed by a nominalization,
following a fairly predictable mapping:

| Conjunction (Verbalstil) | Preposition (Nominalstil) |
| --- | --- |
| nachdem (after) | nach + Nominalisierung |
| weil (because) | wegen + Genitiv |
| wenn (if/when) | bei + Nominalisierung |
| obwohl (although) | trotz + Genitiv |

Examples:

- *Weil die Preise gestiegen sind...* → **Wegen des Anstiegs der Preise...**
- *Wenn die Frist nicht eingehalten wird...* → **Bei Nichteinhaltung der Frist...**
- *Obwohl er krank war, kam er zur Arbeit.* → **Trotz seiner Krankheit kam er zur Arbeit.**

## Why formal German reaches for Nominalstil

Three reasons this style dominates law, academia, administration, and news:

1. **Compression** — a whole clause fits into a short phrase, useful in headlines and dense
   reports.
2. **Impersonality** — nominalizing an action lets the writer omit *who* is doing it, which
   suits legal and bureaucratic neutrality (*bei Nichteinhaltung* doesn't say who might fail to
   comply).
3. **Precision** — a noun can be modified with adjectives and stacked into longer, more exact
   technical phrases than a verb easily allows.

## The other side: readability

German style guides (for clear, reader-friendly writing) actively warn against **overusing**
Nominalstil: long chains of genitives and abstract nouns can bury the actual action of a
sentence and slow the reader down. A good C1 writer can move fluently in both directions —
producing dense Nominalstil where formality calls for it, and unpacking it back into Verbalstil
when clarity matters more. Being able to translate between the two, in both directions, is
exactly the skill this lesson practices.
