At C1, you no longer memorize German vocabulary one word at a time — you learn to **build** it.
German attaches a relatively small set of highly productive prefixes and suffixes to existing
words, and once you know what each piece means, you can decode (and produce) words you have
never explicitly studied.

## Negating prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **un-** | not, opposite of | glücklich (happy) → **un**glücklich (unhappy) |
| **miss-** | wrong, mistaken, failed | verstehen (to understand) → **miss**verstehen (to misunderstand) |

*un-* is by far the most common negating prefix in German, attaching to adjectives (and some
nouns): *möglich → unmöglich*, *bequem → unbequem*, *die Ordnung → die Unordnung*.

## Adjective-forming suffixes

| Suffix | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **-bar** | can be [verb]-ed, -able | essen (to eat) → ess**bar** (edible) |
| **-los** | without, -less | die Arbeit (work) → arbeits**los** (unemployed) |
| **-isch** | of the nature of, related to (often from a noun) | die Politik → polit**isch** (political) |
| **-sam** | tending to, characterized by | schweigen (to be silent) → schweig**sam** (taciturn) |
| **-haft** | having the quality of | der Fehler (mistake) → fehler**haft** (faulty) |
| **-mäßig** | in accordance with, -wise | die Regel (rule) → regel**mäßig** (regular) |

*-bar* is especially productive and predictable: nearly any transitive verb can take it to mean
"capable of being [verb]-ed" — *machbar* (doable), *erreichbar* (reachable), *vermeidbar*
(avoidable).

## Noun-forming suffixes

| Suffix | From | Gender | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **-ung** | verbs | always feminine | bewegen → die Bewegung (movement) |
| **-heit / -keit** | adjectives | always feminine | krank → die Krankheit (illness) |
| **-schaft** | nouns/adjectives | always feminine | der Freund → die Freundschaft (friendship) |
| **-tum** | nouns/adjectives | usually neuter | das Eigentum (property), das Christentum (Christianity) |
| **-nis** | verbs/adjectives | varies | das Ergebnis (result), die Erlaubnis (permission) |

Knowing that *-ung*, *-heit*, *-keit*, and *-schaft* nouns are always feminine removes a whole
category of gender-guessing once you learn to spot the suffix.

## Intensifying and directional prefixes on verbs and nouns

- **erz-** — intensifies, "arch-": der Erzfeind (arch-enemy), erzkonservativ (ultra-conservative)
- **ur-** — original, primal: der Ursprung (origin), uralt (ancient, "primally old")
- **ent-** — removal, reversal, escape: entdecken (dis-cover), entkommen (to escape/get away)
- **zer-** — destruction, breaking apart: zerbrechen (to smash), zerreißen (to tear apart)
- **ver-** — often marks a change of state or a mistake: sprechen → versprechen (to promise, but
  also sich verspechen = to misspeak)

## Why this matters at C1

Recognizing these building blocks turns unfamiliar words from dead ends into puzzles you can
often solve on the spot: seeing *unübersichtlich* for the first time, a C1 learner can parse
*un-* (not) + *übersichtlich* (clear/well-organized, itself from *die Übersicht* + *-lich*) and
land on "confusing, hard to get an overview of" without ever having memorized that exact word.
This is how native speakers handle unfamiliar vocabulary too — not through a bigger memorized
list, but through pattern recognition.
