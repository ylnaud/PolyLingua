You're at a hotel reception desk. Something is wrong with your room, and you need to
raise it without sounding rude. In English, the tool that carries most of the politeness
here isn't a special word — it's a whole grammar structure: the **second conditional**.

## The 5 chunks you need

Don't memorize single words — memorize these complete, ready-to-use phrases:

| English                                                                   | Español                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Excuse me, I'm afraid I have a problem with my room.                      | Disculpe, me temo que tengo un problema con mi habitación.  |
| The heating hasn't been working since this morning.                       | La calefacción no funciona desde esta mañana.               |
| Could you send someone to take a look at the problem?                     | ¿Podría enviar a alguien a revisar el problema?             |
| I'd like a different room, if that's possible.                            | Me gustaría otra habitación, si fuera posible.              |
| Thank you for your understanding — I hope this can be sorted out quickly. | Gracias por su comprensión — espero que se resuelva rápido. |

## The full conversation

> **Receptionist:** Good afternoon, how can I help you?
> **Guest:** Excuse me, I'm afraid I have a problem with my room. The heating hasn't
> been working since this morning.
> **Receptionist:** I'm sorry to hear that. I'll send someone right away.
> **Guest:** I'd like a different room, if that's possible.
> **Receptionist:** That would be fine, if we had a free room — but unfortunately we're
> fully booked today.

## Work it out: why "had" for a present situation?

Look at **"if that's possible"** (real, present tense) next to **"if we had a free
room"** (hypothetical, past-tense form even though it's about right now). Both use "if",
but the verb form changes.

Ask yourself: does the receptionist actually have a free room right now, or is she
softening a "no"? The verb tense is the clue. **"had"** here isn't past time — it's
**distance from reality**. This is the classic pattern of the **second conditional**:

```
If + past simple (had)  →  would + infinitive (would be fine)
```

It describes something unlikely or untrue right now — exactly what the receptionist is
implying: the hotel is probably full. Compare that with "if that's possible", which
stays in the present because the guest is making a real, open request, not describing an
imaginary scenario.

## Practise out loud (shadowing)

Read the whole conversation three times:

1. **Slowly**, isolating "had" every time it marks a hypothetical, so your ear learns to
   catch it as a signal, not just as a past-tense form.
2. **At natural speed**, with your voice rising gently on "...if that's possible" — a
   soft, polite question, not a demand.
3. **Recording yourself**, playing the guest as if this were a real hotel front desk.
   Also write 3-4 sentences about a different hotel problem (wifi, noise, cleaning),
   reusing at least two chunks from above, and save both the recording and the text as if
   you were sending them to a native speaker for correction.
