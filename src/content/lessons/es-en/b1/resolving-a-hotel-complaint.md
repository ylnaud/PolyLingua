The hotel finally offers a solution to your complaint. You need to weigh it, ask for it
in writing, and close the matter politely. This last stage has its own grammar trap: two
ways of talking about "done" that look similar but don't mean the same thing.

## The 5 closing chunks

| English                                                             | Español                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| We've arranged a free upgrade to a suite for your remaining nights. | Le hemos organizado una mejora gratis a suite para las noches que quedan. |
| That sounds like a fair solution, thank you.                        | Eso suena a una solución justa, gracias.                                  |
| Could you confirm that in writing, please?                          | ¿Podría confirmarlo por escrito, por favor?                               |
| As far as I'm concerned, the matter is settled.                     | En lo que a mí respecta, el asunto queda resuelto.                        |
| I really appreciate your quick response.                            | Realmente aprecio su rápida respuesta.                                    |

## The full conversation

> **Receptionist:** We've arranged a free upgrade to a suite for your remaining nights.
> **Guest:** That sounds like a fair solution, thank you. Could you confirm that in
> writing, please?
> **Receptionist:** Of course, I'll email you right away.
> **Guest:** As far as I'm concerned, the matter is settled. I really appreciate your
> quick response.

## Work it out: "we've arranged" vs. "the matter is settled"

Look at **"We've **arranged**..."** (present perfect) and **"the matter **is settled**"**
(present simple passive). Both describe something already done, but they use different
structures.

Ask yourself: in the first sentence, who is doing the arranging? In the second, what is
the sentence actually about — the act of settling, or the state the matter is in now?

- **Present perfect** (have/has + past participle) keeps the **action** and its doer in
  focus: the hotel arranged something, and that action is still relevant now.
- **Present passive as a state** (be + past participle used almost like an adjective)
  shifts the focus entirely to the **result**: the matter, as it stands, is settled — it
  doesn't matter here who settled it or when.

This is the same logic as "the door is open" (a state) versus "someone has opened the
door" (an action). Once you can tell the two apart, you'll know instantly which one fits
a sentence you're building.

## Practise out loud (shadowing)

Read the conversation three times:

1. **Slowly**, marking the difference between "have/has + participle" and "is/are +
   participle" every time they appear.
2. **At natural speed**, with a tone of relief and closure — the voice softens on "the
   matter is settled," like someone genuinely closing the topic.
3. **Recording yourself**, playing the satisfied guest closing the whole conversation.
   Also write how you'd close a different complaint you invented earlier, reusing at
   least two chunks above, and save it with the recording as if sending it to a native
   speaker for correction.
